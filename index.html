00 — Quick Start

Five Moves. First Week.

Everything below is one system split across two tracks. This is where it starts — five things, in order, that cost under $120 total and take less than an hour of actual work.

1
Order
Buy the carriers and the four oils
Cold-pressed castor, jojoba, argan, plus rosemary ct. cineole, lavender, and peppermint. Two amber glass bottles. Full spec in the next section — the chemotype on the rosemary is the one detail you can't get wrong.
2
Test
Patch test before anything else
24 hours behind the ear and on the inner forearm. Peppermint is the ingredient that ends this experiment early for most people. Find out on two drops, not on a full scalp.
3
Start
Begin minoxidil on the beard
5% foam, twice daily, into the skin of the sparse areas only. This is the one item on the list with real clinical evidence behind it. The clock is six months — start it today, not after the oils arrive.
4
Document
Take your day-zero photos
Crown from above, hairline straight on, both temples, beard straight on and both three-quarter profiles. Same room, same light, same distance. Without a baseline you will not be able to tell what worked.
5
Ask
Tell your barber: edges only
Ninety days, top untouched. If you've been getting shape-ups every two to three weeks, that alone can account for the length ceiling — a shape-up blends the perimeter down to match, removing roughly what you grew since the last visit.

What This Protocol Will and Won't Do

The oil blend improves scalp condition, cuts breakage, and seals ends. That's real and it's measurable. What it will not do is extend your anagen phase or fill a beard patch. No topical oil has human data on either. The minoxidil is what addresses those — the oil is the supporting cast, not the lead.

01 — Ingredient Spec

Buy Right, Or Don't Bother

The essential oil market runs on adulteration. Latin name, chemotype, origin, distillation date, and batch-specific GC/MS you can pull up yourself — anything short of that is a bottle of something, not a bottle of what you paid for.

IngredientCastor Oil
Required SpecRicinus communis — cold-pressed, unrefined, hexane-free, amber glass
VerifyRicinoleic acid 85–90%. Pale-to-golden amber. Uniform pale color means it's been refined.

Required SpecSalvia rosmarinus — chemotype 1,8-cineole. Not verbenone. Not camphor.

IngredientPeppermint

Automatic Reject

Fatty acid esters or synthetic glycols on any essential oil COA. Those don't come from the plant — that's carrier dilution being sold to you at essential oil prices.

On Ricin

Ricin is a water-soluble protein that stays behind in the pressed seed mash. It is not present in properly processed castor oil. Not a concern, not something to research further.

03 — Mixing & Patch Test

Build It Right

Order of operations matters more than people expect. Water shortens shelf life, aeration accelerates oxidation, and oxidized oils are the leading cause of new-onset skin sensitization.

1
Prep
Sanitize the bottle
2 oz amber or cobalt glass with a dropper. Isopropyl rinse, air dry completely. Any water left behind cuts the shelf life significantly.
2
Pour
Jojoba first, castor second
Jojoba is thin and pours clean. Castor is viscous — pour slow, let it settle, then top up to volume.
3
Drop
Essential oils last
Count directly into the bottle. Rosemary, then lavender, then peppermint.
4
Combine
Invert, don't shake
Cap and invert slowly 30–40 times. Hard shaking aerates the blend and starts the oxidation clock early.
Label with blend name, EO percentage, and mix date. Rest 24 hours before first use.
5
Required
Patch test — 24 hoursDo Not Skip
Two drops behind one ear, two on the inner forearm. Leave uncovered, don't wash. Check at 1 hour, 12 hours, 24 hours.
Abort on: persistent redness, raised bumps, itching past 20 minutes, or burning. A brief cooling tingle from peppermint in the first 5–10 minutes is expected and is not a failure. If it fails, drop peppermint to zero and re-test — it's the usual culprit.

04 — Scalp Protocol

Type 3 Coils, Length Retained

Afro-textured hair sheds less and breaks more. Shed strands in other hair types come out full-length with a club bulb; afro-textured hair frequently leaves as short fragments with longitudinal fissures. That means breakage, not shedding — and breakage is the thing you can actually control.

2–3× Per Week
Not daily — castor-based oils build up and occlude follicles
Step 01
Start with dry, unwashed hair. Wet hair blocks penetration and dilutes the blend.
Step 02
Warm the bottle in a mug of hot tap water for 3–4 minutes. Never microwave — it degrades the essential oils.
Step 03
Section into 6–8 parts with a rat-tail comb.
Step 04
Apply to the scalp only, not the lengths. Roughly 1 mL — about 20 drops — per session total. You should not be saturating the hair.
Step 05
Massage 4–5 minutes. Fingerpads, not nails. Small circles, steady pressure. The mechanical massage has its own independent evidence — don't rush this one.
Step 06
Leave on 30 minutes minimum, or overnight under a satin bonnet. Castor stains fabric permanently.

The Washout — Where Most People Go Wrong

Apply shampoo to dry, oiled hair first. Work it all the way through before any water touches your head. Then add water and emulsify.

Castor will not lift if you wet the hair first — that's why people end up washing four times and still feel coated. Two shampoo passes, and use a clarifying or sulfate shampoo for this specific washout even if you normally avoid them.

Retention — The Part That Actually Moves Length

  • Seal the ends. The last two inches after every wash, while damp. Castor plus jojoba, no essential oils needed down there — the ends are the oldest part of your hair and the most fragile.
  • Detangle damp with conditioner in. Ends first, work upward. Wide-tooth comb or fingers only, never dry. The slip is doing the work, not the water.
  • Satin every night. Cotton friction is a silent tax on exactly the section you're trying to keep.
  • Dust the ends every 8–10 weeks. Feels backwards when you're chasing length. It isn't — splits migrate up the shaft, so you cut a quarter inch now or lose two later.
  • Deep condition weekly, rotate protein. Three moisture treatments to one protein. Moisture-only leaves the shaft soft and stretchy; protein-only makes it brittle.

Stop and See a Dermatologist If

Crown thinning with the hairline holding, plus itching, burning, tenderness, or symptoms that worsen after a haircut. That presentation points toward CCCA, which is scarring and permanent — and in men it gets routinely misread as ordinary pattern loss. Six months of oiling while it progresses is six months you don't get back.

Gradual Thin Spots, Filled In

The Oil — Application

  • After showering, on a damp beard
  • 3–6 drops depending on length, warmed between the palms
  • Work into the skin underneath first, then out through the hair
  • Comb through to distribute · Daily is appropriate at 1%
  • Always after minoxidil has fully absorbed — never before

On Razor Bumps

Pseudofolliculitis barbae affects as many as 83% of adult Black men, driven by follicle curvature. This oil is not a treatment for it — what it does is keep the skin under the beard from drying and flaking, which is a contributing factor. If there are active inflamed bumps or pustules, don't oil through them. Stop shaving that area until they clear.

06 — Minoxidil

The One With Real Evidence

Off-label — minoxidil is FDA-approved for scalp only. Systemic absorption is real, so talk to a physician or pharmacist first, particularly with any blood pressure or cardiac history.

5% Topical Foam
Generic acceptable · Roughly $25 per 3-month supply
Amount≈ half a capful
FrequencyTwice daily
TargetSparse areas only
Skin stateClean, completely dry
Absorption window4 hours minimum
Evening doseWash off before sleep

Foam over the liquid solution — the solution's propylene glycol irritates facial skin significantly more, and worse on textured skin already prone to flaking. Washing off the evening dose prevents pillow transfer to the eyes.

Timeline

Weeks 1–8
No visible change. Some shedding is possible and normal — resting follicles being pushed into growth simultaneously. This is when most people quit. Don't.
Weeks 8–12
Fine, wispy new hairs appearing in the sparse areas.
Months 3–6
Progressive thickening and darkening.
Month 6
Decision point. No change by now means it's unlikely to work for you. Stopping is reasonable.
Months 12–18
The window in which non-terminal hairs convert to terminal. This is why you run it longer than six months.

On Permanence — Beard Differs From Scalp

Scalp hair in pattern loss is actively suppressed by DHT, so stopping lets the underlying process resume. Beard follicles are stimulated by DHT, not attacked by it — there's no opposing process waiting to resume.

The practical rule: hairs that went terminal — thick, dark, pigmented, matching the surrounding beard — generally persist. Hairs that stayed fine and wispy were treatment-dependent and will be lost. Taper over 2–3 months rather than stopping cold; whatever's still there three months after the last application is durable.

Worth being straight about: no study has formally tested beard retention after discontinuation. This is mechanism plus consistent anecdotal report, not published data.

07 — Microneedling

Optional. Biggest Multiplier.

The supporting evidence is scalp, not beard — the landmark trial ran microneedling plus minoxidil against minoxidil alone and found roughly four times the new hair count. No beard-specific microneedling trial exists. Same skin, same mechanism, extrapolated.

0.5 mm · Once Weekly
Roller or stamp · $15–40 for a quality device
01
Clean face, clean hands.
02
Roll each section 4–5 passes in one direction, lift, repeat perpendicular, then diagonal. Always lift before changing direction — dragging tears skin.
03
Light, even pressure. Endpoint is slight pinkness, not bleeding.
04
Total time 2–5 minutes. Once per week, not more.
05
Soak the device in 70% isopropyl for 5–10 minutes, air dry, store in its case. Replace every 8–12 weeks — dull needles tear instead of puncture.

Do not exceed 0.5 mm at home. Longer needles are clinical-use devices and the depth is what determines whether this is a treatment or an injury.

The 24-Hour Rule — Non-Negotiable

No minoxidil for 24 hours after rolling. Fresh micro-channels dramatically increase absorption, which raises the risk of irritation, burning, and systemic effects including palpitations and dizziness. Skip both doses on rolling day, resume the next day.

Beard oil: wait a minimum of 4–8 hours, or just apply the next morning.

Do Not Microneedle If

You're keloid-prone or have any history of raised scarring on the jaw, neck, or face. Also skip it over active inflamed bumps, pustules, or broken skin, and any week the skin hasn't fully recovered from the previous session.

09 — Safety & Storage

Hard Limits, No Exceptions

Hard Stops

Topical use only — do not ingest any of these formulas. Keep away from eyes, inner ears, and mucous membranes. If peppermint gets in the eye, flush with a plain carrier oil, not water — menthol is oil-soluble. Do not use the peppermint-containing blends on or near the face of children under 6.

Consult a Physician Before Use If Any Apply

  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding — rosemary carries standard precautionary advice
  • Seizure disorder or epilepsy — 1,8-cineole and camphor precaution
  • G6PD deficiency — menthol precaution
  • Active scalp psoriasis, eczema, seborrheic dermatitis, or broken skin
  • Current use of a prescription scalp treatment — don't layer without clearance
  • Any blood pressure or cardiac history, before starting minoxidil

Not Phototoxic

None of these four oils cause sun sensitivity. No timing restrictions around sun exposure — which is a separate question from your SPF routine, which stays exactly as it is.

Mix Small, Mix Often

2 oz batches, never larger. Every time you open a bottle you introduce oxygen — a big batch spends months oxidizing, and oxidized oils are the primary cause of new-onset skin sensitization.

Do not refrigerate the finished blend. Castor thickens badly and condensation forms on the bottle as it returns to room temperature. Discard if it smells sharp, rancid, or noticeably different from mix day.

10 — Tracking

Measure It, Don't Guess

Coily hair shrinks 50–75%. Two inches of real growth can show as under an inch of visible change, and at short lengths the month-to-month difference is effectively invisible to the naked eye. "Feels like" is not a measurement.

Wet-Stretch Measurement
The only way to know whether you have a ceiling or a perception problem
Setup
Pick one spot — the crown works. Same spot every single time.
Method
Wet the hair. Stretch a single strand flat against a ruler. Measure root to tip. Photograph the ruler. Log the number.
Cadence
Day 0, day 30, day 60, day 90. No cuts to the top during the window.
Reading it
Gaining roughly 0.9 cm per month means you were never plateauing — you just couldn't see it. Flat across all three means a real ceiling, and that's worth a dermatologist.

Photo Protocol

  • Scalp: crown from directly above, hairline straight on, both temples in profile
  • Beard: straight on, plus both three-quarter profiles
  • Same room, same light source, same time of day, same camera distance
  • Hair dry and in the same style each time
  • Repeat every 4 weeks — compare month 3 to day 0, never consecutive weeks

Also Log

Shed count on wash day. Scalp condition — itch, flaking, tenderness on a 1 to 5 scale. Any change in medication, diet, or stress. Inconsistent lighting and missing context are why most people can't tell whether anything worked.

The Honest Ceiling

If the 90-day measurement comes back flat, you may simply have a short anagen phase. That's a real thing and nothing is wrong with you. At that point the move is styling to the length you actually keep — a well-shaped short cut maintained sharp beats fighting your own follicle cycle for years.
